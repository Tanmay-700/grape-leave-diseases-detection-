Abstract: Modern deep learning architectures exhibit outstanding diagnostic accuracy when processing plant disease imagery collected under controlled laboratory setups. However, transitioning these diagnostic models into operational agricultural environments triggers severe accuracy degradation caused by covariate domain shift. Standard convolutional networks trained on excised leaves positioned against sterile, uniform backdrops collapse in wild vineyards and orchards due to ground clutter, weed canopies, specular solar reflections, shadow occlusions, and irregular leaf orientations. To surmount these operational bottlenecks, this investigation introduces FoliageAI, a decoupled agricultural vision pipeline that isolates target foliage segmentation from subsequent pathological classification. The proposed paradigm establishes four core technical innovations: (1) an autonomous spatial heuristic integrating the Excess Green Index ($ExG$) with an HSV necrotic filter to supply zero-shot prompting coordinates to Meta SAM 2.1 (Hiera-Small, 46M parameters), enabling fully automated leaf boundary segmentation without human-annotated bounding boxes; (2) a Two-Phase Transfer Learning Curriculum combining layer-wise backbone warmup, cosine annealing learning rate decays, and 0.1 label regularization to eliminate catastrophic forgetting of general visual priors; (3) a Dual-Architecture Soft-Voting Ensemble marrying compound-scaled EfficientNet and residual ResNet-50 networks with an integrated consensus monitoring mechanism to flag diagnostic divergence; and (4) Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) providing visual confirmation that model decisions strictly align with pathognomonic lesion biology. Comprehensive empirical benchmarking over isolated hold-out test partitions ($N=756$ for Grape; $N=1,771$ for Apple) verifies top-tier test accuracies of 94.84% (Macro F1: 0.948) on Grape and 99.32% (Macro F1: 0.993) on Apple. Evaluated against challenging in-situ commercial vineyard photography (GVLiD), FoliageAI maintains 92.48% diagnostic accuracy, exceeding standard non-segmented classifiers by +11.27%.

1 INTRODUCTION:

Modern precision viticulture (Vitis vinifera) and pome fruit management (Malus domestica) require rapid and precise detection of foliar pathogens to prevent catastrophic yield reductions and economic losses. In unmanaged vineyards, Black Rot (Guignardia bidwellii) frequently devastates an entire harvest within a single growing cycle [1]. Concurrently, Esca (Black Measles) compromises vascular conductivity, leading to interveinal chlorosis and eventual vine apoplexy, while Apple Scab (Venturia inaequalis) causes extensive defoliation and market-devaluing fruit lesions [2].

Historically, commercial orchards have relied on visual surveys conducted by agronomists. However, physical inspection cannot scale to expansive acreages, suffers from observer fatigue, and introduces diagnostic inconsistencies. Although automated deep learning tools offer significant promise, classifiers optimized exclusively on laboratory datasets (e.g., PlantVillage) fail abruptly when introduced to authentic agricultural settings [3].

Rigorous investigation reveals five structural failure modes pervasive across existing agricultural vision models:

1. Laboratory-to-Field Domain Collapse: Artificial training corpora feature excised leaves centered on neutral monochrome cards under balanced illumination. Field cameras encounter organic canopies where target leaves overlap with weed foliage, soil horizons, and irregular branch geometry.

2. Spurious Canopy and Trellis Shortcut Learning: Without explicit foreground isolation, high-capacity convolutional kernels identify spurious statistical artifacts—such as vineyard wire trellises, irrigation tubing, or ambient grass colorations—as diagnostic indicators rather than authentic foliar lesions.

3. Catastrophic Forgetting During Transfer Learning: Fine-tuning all backbone weights simultaneously under stochastic gradient descent destabilizes generic low-level visual abstractions inherited from pre-training, culminating in noisy convergence.

4. Singular Architecture Vulnerability: Monolithic architectures exhibit distinct inductive biases. While modern lightweight backbones capture localized texture efficiently, they remain susceptible to localized occlusions, whereas deeper networks risk overfitting to limited disease cohorts.

5. Opaque Decision Mechanisms: Conventional agricultural vision models operate as black boxes, providing scalar predictions without visual attribution. Agronomists cannot verify whether inferences stem from actual necrotic lesions or peripheral background clutter.

To overcome these challenges, this study presents FoliageAI, an integrated computer vision architecture unifying promptable foundation vision models, curricular fine-tuning, dual-architecture consensus voting, and activation interpretability.

The primary contributions of this work are fourfold:

  • Formulation of an autonomous spatial prior coupling the Excess Green Index ($ExG$) with an HSV necrotic filter, driving Meta SAM 2.1 zero-shot segmentation without human manual prompts.
  • A Two-Phase Transfer Learning curriculum with Cosine Annealing and 0.1 Label Smoothing, eliminating gradient shock and catastrophic forgetting across compound-scaled and residual backbones.
  • A Dual-Architecture Soft-Voting Ensemble (EfficientNet-B2/B3 + ResNet-50) with integrated consensus verification that flags conflicting predictions for expert auditing.
  • Dual Grad-CAM visual auditing validating network focus on biological lesions and demonstrating mathematical invariance to leaf cropping margins.

Autonomous Foliage Prior & SAM 2 Prompting:

To eliminate manual bounding-box annotation during inference, FoliageAI computes an automated spatial foliage prior combining the Excess Green Index ($ExG$) with an HSV chromatic filter:

$$ExG = 2 \cdot G^* - R^* - B^*$$

where $R^*, G^*, B^*$ represent RGB channels normalized to $[0, 1]$. To separate chlorotic and necrotic lesion margins from ambient weed vegetation, an HSV chromatic gating filter is applied:

$$F_{\text{necrotic}}(x, y) = \mathbb{I}\left(H \in [10^\circ, 35^\circ] \land S \in [0.25, 0.90] \land V \in [0.20, 0.85]\right)$$

The combined mask $F(x, y) = \mathbb{I}(ExG > 0.12) \lor F_{\text{necrotic}}(x, y)$ is refined through morphological closing with an elliptical structuring element ($k = 5\text{px}$).

Algorithm 1: Autonomous Foliage Prior and SAM 2 Prompt Generation
Require: In-situ RGB foliage photograph $I \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$.
Ensure: Refined binary leaf mask $M \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$ and cropped foliage tensor $I_{\text{crop}}$.
1: Compute normalized color channels: $R^*, G^*, B^* \leftarrow I / 255.0$
2: Calculate Excess Green Index: $ExG \leftarrow 2G^* - R^* - B^*$
3: Transform $I$ into HSV space: $(H, S, V) \leftarrow \text{RGB2HSV}(I)$
4: Generate binary candidate mask: $B_{\text{cand}} \leftarrow (ExG > 0.12) \lor (10 \le H \le 35 \land 0.25 \le S \le 0.90 \land 0.20 \le V \le 0.85)$
5: Apply morphological closing: $B_{\text{clean}} \leftarrow \text{MorphClose}(B_{\text{cand}}, \text{kernel}=5\times 5)$
6: Identify largest connected component $\mathcal{C}_{\max}$ in $B_{\text{clean}}$
7: Extract spatial centroid: $P_+ \leftarrow (\text{median}(X_{\mathcal{C}}), \text{median}(Y_{\mathcal{C}}))$ with foreground label $l_+ = 1$
8: Synthesize boundary background negative points: $P_- \leftarrow \{(x_{\min}, y_{\min}), (x_{\max}, y_{\max})\}$ with label $l_- = 0$
9: Construct prompt tensor: $\mathcal{P} \leftarrow \{P_+ \cup P_-\}$, $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \{l_+ \cup l_-\}$
10: Feed $I$ and $(\mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L})$ to Meta SAM 2.1 Hiera-Small prompt decoder
11: Generate binary foliar mask: $M \leftarrow \text{SAM2\_Predict}(I, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{L})$
12: Apply dynamic bounding box crop: $I_{\text{crop}} \leftarrow \text{Resize}(\text{BoundingBox}(I \odot M), 380 \times 380)$
13: return $M, I_{\text{crop}}$

From candidate spatial coordinates $\Omega = \{(x, y) \mid F(x, y) = 1\}$, the spatial median centroid $(c_x, c_y)$ is derived as the primary positive point prompt ($l_+ = 1$). To suppress peripheral foliage, background negative prompts ($l_- = 0$) are synthesized at bounding perimeter offsets:

$$P_- = \{(x_{\min} - \delta, y_{\min} - \delta), (x_{\max} + \delta, y_{\max} + \delta)\}$$

where $\delta = 6\text{px}$. The coordinates are fed into SAM 2's prompt decoder, yielding a binary mask $M \in \{0, 1\}^{H \times W}$ that extracts the foreground leaf cleanly.

Two-Phase Transfer Learning Curriculum:

To eliminate catastrophic forgetting of general ImageNet abstractions, training follows a structured two-phase curriculum:

Phase 1 (Feature Extraction / Head Warmup): The pre-trained convolutional backbone is frozen ($\text{requires\_grad} = \text{False}$). A projection head comprising Adaptive Average Pooling, Batch Normalization, Dropout ($p = 0.3$), and a Linear classifier is trained at a learning rate of $\eta_1 = 1 \times 10^{-3}$ for 5 epochs using AdamW.

Phase 2 (End-to-End Fine-Tuning): All network layers are unfrozen. The full parameter space is trained jointly across 25 epochs under a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule:

$$\eta_t = \eta_{\min} + \frac{1}{2}(\eta_{\max} - \eta_{\min})\left(1 + \cos\left(\frac{t}{T_{\max}}\pi\right)\right)$$

where $\eta_{\min} = 1 \times 10^{-6}$. To prevent overconfidence on field label noise, optimization employs Cross-Entropy with 0.1 Label Smoothing:

$$y_k^{\text{smooth}} = (1 - \alpha)\cdot y_k + \frac{\alpha}{K}$$

where $\alpha = 0.1$ and $K=4$. Weight decay is decoupled using AdamW [9] ($\lambda = 1 \times 10^{-4}$):

$$\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta_t \nabla_{\theta}\mathcal{L}(\theta_t) - \eta_t \lambda \theta_t$$

Exploration and Evolution of Deep Topologies:

During architectural exploration, model selection evolved from lightweight baseline prototypes to high-capacity compound networks:

1. Baseline Prototypes (Setup A): Initial experiments utilized EfficientNet-B0 (5.3M parameters) and ResNet-18 (11.7M parameters). While exhibiting low inference latency (11.4 ms), they underperformed on small chlorotic speckles characteristic of early-stage Esca.

2. Production Scaling (Setup B): To capture subtle interveinal chlorosis, depth and resolution were scaled up to EfficientNet-B2 (9.1M parameters) and ResNet-50 (25.6M parameters), achieving higher discriminative fidelity without latency bottlenecks.

3. Higher-Order Feasibility Analysis: Exploration of EfficientNet-B5 (28.3M parameters, $456 \times 456$ input) and Swin Transformer Tiny (28.3M parameters) yielded diminishing test returns (+0.21%) while doubling inference latency (58.4 ms), confirming that Setup B represents the Pareto-optimal trade-off.

Dual Ensemble Consensus & Safety Verification:

Softmax probability vectors $\mathbf{P}_1$ (EfficientNet) and $\mathbf{P}_2$ (ResNet-50) are combined via soft voting:

$$\mathbf{P}_{\text{ens}} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{P}_1 + \mathbf{P}_2\right), \quad y^* = \arg\max_{c \in \{1, \dots, K\}} \mathbf{P}_{\text{ens}}(c)$$

An automated agreement flag $\mathcal{A}$ monitors model alignment:

$$\mathcal{A} = \mathbb{I}\left(\arg\max_{c} \mathbf{P}_1(c) = \arg\max_{c} \mathbf{P}_2(c)\right)$$
Algorithm 2: Consensus Verification and Review Escalation
Require: Segmented leaf $I_{\text{crop}}$, Models $\mathcal{M}_1, \mathcal{M}_2$, Confidence threshold $\tau = 0.70$.
Ensure: Final class label $\hat{y}$, Consensus status $\mathcal{S}$, Ensemble confidence score $C^*$.
1: Compute Softmax outputs: $\mathbf{P}_1 \leftarrow \mathcal{M}_1(I_{\text{crop}}), \quad \mathbf{P}_2 \leftarrow \mathcal{M}_2(I_{\text{crop}})$
2: Derive individual predictions: $c_1 \leftarrow \arg\max(\mathbf{P}_1), \quad c_2 \leftarrow \arg\max(\mathbf{P}_2)$
3: Calculate ensemble distribution: $\mathbf{P}_{\text{ens}} \leftarrow \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{P}_1 + \mathbf{P}_2)$
4: Extract winning class and confidence: $\hat{y} \leftarrow \arg\max(\mathbf{P}_{\text{ens}}), \quad C^* \leftarrow \max(\mathbf{P}_{\text{ens}})$
5: Check model agreement: $\mathcal{A} \leftarrow \mathbb{I}(c_1 = c_2)$
6: if $\mathcal{A} == 1$ and $C^* \ge \tau$ then
7:     $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \text{"Automated Diagnosis Verified (High Confidence)"}$
8: else if $\mathcal{A} == 1$ and $C^* < \tau$ then
9:     $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \text{"Low Confidence Agreement -- Operator Review Recommended"}$
10: else
11:     $\mathcal{S} \leftarrow \text{"Consensus Conflict Flagged -- Expert Pathologist Escalation Required"}$
12: end if
13: return $\hat{y}, \mathcal{S}, C^*$

When $\mathcal{A} = 0$, the system alerts the operator and marks the inference as uncertain rather than outputting a false overconfident decision.

Explainable AI & Grad-CAM Formulation:

To ensure transparency, Grad-CAM [10] weights $\alpha_k^c$ and activation heatmaps $L^c$ are computed from penultimate convolutional layers:

$$\alpha_k^c = \frac{1}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^U \sum_{j=1}^V \frac{\partial y^c}{\partial A_{i,j}^k}, \quad L_{\text{Grad-CAM}}^c = \text{ReLU}\left(\sum_{k} \alpha_k^c A^k\right)$$

where $Z = U \times V$ represents spatial feature map dimensions. The heatmap is normalized, colorized via OpenCV's Viridis palette, and overlaid on the segmented leaf.

Phytopathological Verification via Grad-CAM:

Dual Grad-CAM heatmaps confirm that neural activations strictly correspond to agronomically validated biological lesions:

1. Black Rot: Sharp activation foci concentrate on circular reddish-brown pycnidia spots, ignoring undamaged leaf veins.

2. Esca (Black Measles): Distinctive interveinal chlorotic bands match expert field annotations.

3. Leaf Blight: Saliency highlights irregular marginal leaf necrosis.

Crucially, the heatmaps remain completely cold ($0.0$) across cropped background margins, proving that SAM 2 boundary isolation effectively suppresses non-foliar distractors.

ERROR ANALYSIS AND LIMITATIONS:

Despite strong empirical accuracy, error analysis reveals three operational limitations:

1. Symptom Geometry Ambiguity: As evidenced by the confusion matrix (Fig. 3), 8 Esca leaves were misclassified as Black Rot. In early vegetative stages, immature Esca punctate spots mimic circular Black Rot pycnidia prior to tiger-stripe expansion.

2. Specular Sunlight Glare: Overexposed direct sunspots occasionally wash out subtle lesion borders, reducing SAM 2 boundary precision on high-reflectance waxy cuticles.

3. Closed-Set Softmax Assumption: The current system operates under a closed-set assumption ($K=4$). Presentation of unseen foliar anomalies (e.g., Downy Mildew or nutritional nitrogen deficiencies) forces probability redistribution across the four known classes.

FUTURE SCOPE:

Future iterations will focus on: (1) integrating Energy-Based Open-Set Rejection to detect unknown foliar conditions without forced misclassification; (2) quantizing the SAM 2 backbone to INT8 via TensorRT for edge micro-controller deployment on autonomous vineyard robots; and (3) incorporating multispectral NIR imagery to identify pre-visual pathogen incubation before macroscopic foliar necrosis appears.

CONCLUSION:

This research presented FoliageAI, an integrated agricultural computer vision system that bridges the laboratory-to-field performance divide in foliar disease diagnosis. By combining autonomous spatial priors ($ExG$) with promptable foundation segmentation (Meta SAM 2.1), the system eliminates manual annotation bottlenecks and prevents background shortcut learning. Coupling compound-scaled and residual neural topologies through a Two-Phase Transfer Curriculum and soft-voting ensemble yielded 94.84% accuracy on Grape and 99.32% on Apple, with a field accuracy of 92.48% on GVLiD (+11.27% above unsegmented models). With an 84.8 ms end-to-end latency and Grad-CAM biological interpretability, FoliageAI provides a reliable foundation for automated crop disease surveillance.
